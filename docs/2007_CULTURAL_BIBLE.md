# BIG BANG LORE: 2007 Cultural Bible

Bengaluru, **1 January to 15 June 2007**. This is the reference for dialogue, props, signage, posters, TV and radio, wardrobe and world-building. It is meant to keep the period honest.

## How to read the tags

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[V]** | Checked against a source while writing this document. The source is listed at the bottom. |
| **[K]** | Widely documented general knowledge, not re-checked line by line in this pass. Verify before it appears as an on-screen fact such as a newspaper headline, date or number. |
| **[F]** | Fiction made for this game. Never present it as history. |
| **[?]** | Uncertain. Do not use as fact until checked. |

**Rule:** a real person, institution or event may appear as *texture* or in a *clearly fictionalised scene*. The game never puts invented quotes, private statements or invented incidents in the mouths of real people as if they were records. The opening card says so on screen.

**Narrative use:** the months between the reel running out and the release are the story's clock. The audio launch in April and the run-up to 15 June are the natural public beats. The Bangalore release protests are real period texture. Handle them carefully: the language politics are real and both sides have real feelings. Do not caricature either side.

## 2. Rajinikanth and fan culture

- Rajinikanth was born Shivaji Rao Gaekwad in Bangalore and worked as a BTS bus conductor before cinema. **[K]** Older Bangaloreans say "our boy" about him. Kannada and Tamil identities around him are complicated, and characters can argue about this.
- **Fan associations (rasigar mandrams):** registered clubs with presidents, membership cards, portrait banners, and first-day first-show rituals: milk abhishekam on cut-outs, garlands, firecrackers, distributing sweets. **[K]** Club rivalries over who puts up the biggest banner or gets the most tickets are a believable source of friction between men like Raja and Lal. Any specific club in the game is **[F]**.
- **Address:** "Thalaiva" (leader), "Thalaivar" (third person), "Superstar". Fans quote *Padayappa* (1999), *Baasha* (1995), *Muthu* (1995) and *Chandramukhi* (2005; a huge comeback hit). **[K]**
- In 2007 the props are fan-club ID cards, laminated photos in wallets, calendars with his picture, and cassette and VCD covers.

## 3. Cinema, early 2007

**Kannada**
- *Mungaru Male* (Ganesh, Pooja Gandhi; dir. Yogaraj Bhat) released on **29 December 2006** and became a phenomenon through 2007, with record long runs and a multiplex record. On 1 January 2007 it was three days old. Its full scale only became clear over the following months. **[V]**
- Other stars and names of the period: Puneeth Rajkumar, Shivarajkumar, Sudeep, Upendra, Darshan, Ganesh ("Golden Star" comes after *Mungaru Male*), Ramya. *Duniya* (Vijay, early 2007). **[K]**; check exact dates before using them on posters.
- Gandhinagar, near Majestic, is the Kannada film district: distributors' offices and hand-painted hoardings. Theatres include Kapali, Santosh, Sagar, Triveni, Menaka and Kempegowda. **[K]**

**Tamil**
- *Pokkiri* (Vijay) is a Pongal 2007 release (January). *Paruthiveeran* (Feb 2007). *Chennai 600028* and *Unnale Unnale* (spring 2007). **[K]**
- Bangalore theatres that ran Tamil films include Lavanya and others around Shivajinagar and the Cantonment. **[K]**; check the names before signage uses them.

**Telugu and Hindi:** *Desamuduru* (Allu Arjun, Jan 2007); *Guru* (Abhishek Bachchan and Aishwarya Rai, 12 Jan 2007; Mani Ratnam; A. R. Rahman music). *Dhoom 2* (Nov 2006) was still playing in some halls in early 2007. *Namastey London* came in March 2007. **[K]**

## 4. Music in the air (Jan to Jun 2007)

- *Mungaru Male* songs ("Anisuthide", the title song) are everywhere in Bangalore by spring. **[K]**
- *Guru*: "Tere Bina", "Barso Re". **[K]**
- *Sivaji* songs only exist publicly **after the audio launch (around 2 April)**. Before that, nobody in the game hums them. **[V]** (the date)
- **Game rule (unchanged):** no background score while walking, talking or on missions. Music plays only on a vehicle radio, and only the authorised tracks the user supplies. Real commercial recordings are never bundled.

## 5. Television, radio and advertising

- Channels: Sun TV, Udaya TV (Kannada), Jaya TV, Zee, Star Plus, Doordarshan, and Aaj Tak and NDTV for news. Cricket is on Doordarshan and the rights holder's channel. **[K]**
- *Kaun Banega Crorepati* season 3 with Shah Rukh Khan starts in January 2007. **[K]**
- FM radio: Radio City and Radio Mirchi are in Bangalore by 2006–07, and AIR / Akashvani is still a staple. **[K]**; check which stations were on air in Jan 2007 before naming them.
- Ads people quote: Hutch's pug ("Wherever you go, our network follows"). **Hutch was still Hutch until it rebranded to Vodafone in September 2007**, which is after the story ends. **[K]**

## 6. Phones, the internet and technology

- Phones: Nokia 1100 and 1600 (the indestructible ones), Nokia 3310 (older), Nokia 6600 and 6630, N70 and N73 (aspirational), Motorola RAZR V3, Sony Ericsson W810i (Walkman), and cheap Reliance CDMA handsets. Networks: Airtel, Hutch, BSNL CellOne, Reliance, Tata Indicom. There are missed-call signals, SMS packs, ringtone and caller-tune culture, and Bluetooth file sharing. **[K]**
- STD/ISD/PCO booths are still common. Landlines carry STD codes (Bangalore 080). **[K]**
- Internet cafés (Sify iway, Reliance Web World, plenty of local ones) are where people log into **Orkut**, the dominant social network in India in 2007, with scraps and testimonials. Yahoo Messenger, Rediffmail and Gmail invites are around. Facebook is not yet mainstream in India. **[K]**
- Media: VCDs and DVDs (pirated and original), cassettes, MP3 CDs, CRT TVs, cable operators (local cable networks), and the arrival of DTH (Dish TV, then Tata Sky). **[K]**

## 7. Cricket and sport

- India's captain is Rahul Dravid and the coach is Greg Chappell (who leaves after the World Cup). Sachin, Sourav, Sehwag, Yuvraj, Dhoni (long hair), Zaheer, Kumble (a Bangalore hero). **[K]**
- The World Cup exit in March 2007 dominates conversation. The first T20 World Cup (September 2007) and the IPL (2008) are **after** our period, so characters cannot know about them. **[K]**

## 8. Politics and the city

- Karnataka's Chief Minister is H. D. Kumaraswamy (JD(S)–BJP coalition, from Feb 2006), with B. S. Yeddyurappa as Deputy CM. The rotation crisis comes in October 2007, after our period. **[K]**
- "Bengaluru": the state moved to change the city's English name around Suvarna Karnataka (50th Rajyotsava, 1 Nov 2006). **[?]** The official change came much later. In 2007 most people say and write **Bangalore** in English. The game's UI uses "Bengaluru" as its own voice. Signage of the period says "Bangalore".
- The IT boom and the new city: Whitefield and Electronic City offices, call-centre cabs (Qualis and Tata Sumo), real-estate hoardings, flyovers going up, the Hosur Road elevated expressway under construction, and Namma Metro planned but not built (construction began 2007). The new airport at Devanahalli is under construction; flights still use HAL airport. **[K]**; check months before stating them as events.

## 9. Streets and wheels

- Auto-rickshaws in 2007 are **black and yellow**. LPG was mandatory for autos renewing registration from November 2002. The green-and-yellow livery for four-stroke LPG autos came in **late 2009**, so the game's autos stay black and yellow. **[V]**
- Two-wheelers: Bajaj Pulsar 150/180, Hero Honda Splendor and Passion, CBZ, TVS Victor and Star City, Yamaha RX100 (still loved, long out of production), Kinetic Honda, TVS Scooty, Bajaj Chetak. **[K]**
- Cars: Maruti 800, Alto, Zen, Wagon R, Esteem; Hyundai Santro; Tata Indica (the taxi of choice); Toyota Qualis (being replaced by the Innova); Tata Sumo; Ambassador (government). **[K]**
- BMTC buses: red ordinary buses, with Volvo "Vajra" buses newly on some routes. **[K]**; check dates.

## 10. Wardrobe and looks

- Men: half-sleeve shirts worn out (checks and prints) or tucked into pleated trousers; jeans with a wide belt; sandals, Bata or canvas shoes, chappals; a gold chain for the confident; thick moustaches; side-parted or "Rajini style" swept hair; a watch on the left wrist. Students carry backpacks.
- Women: salwar kameez with dupatta for students; saree for working women and elders; plaited hair with jasmine; a few in jeans and kurti on campus.
- Film unit: crew in T-shirts and jeans or checked shirts; light boys in vests or banians; the assistant director with a walkie-talkie or call sheet, shouting; private security in safari suits or black T-shirts.
- **Raja and Lal (proposed, [F]):** Raja wears a maroon checked shirt, untucked, with jeans and canvas shoes, side-parted thick hair and a full moustache: lean, quick, restless. Lal wears a dark full-sleeve shirt with beige trousers and chappals, curly hair, a short beard and a gold watch: broad, slow, chest-out.

## 11. Language and how people talk

- Bangalore is multilingual: Kannada, Tamil, Telugu, Urdu/Dakhni and English mixed mid-sentence. A Tamil-speaking Bangalorean switches to Kannada with Kannadiga friends and back without noticing.
- Kannada of the street: "maga" (literally "son", meaning buddy, or an insult depending on tone), "guru", "swalpa adjust maadi", "sakkath", "bombat", "en guru", "saaku" (enough), "hogi" (go). "Sule maga" is a crude insult (son of a whore). The game uses it the way the brief asks, as Raja's angry half-sentence, not as a punchline.
- **Game rule: every local character (Raja, Lal, Umashankar, students, crowd, security, police, friends) speaks Kannada.** Only the film unit (a Chennai crew) speaks Tamil or English. Fans still chant "Thalaiva!": the word is universal fan usage, not a switch of language.
- 2007 Bangalore Kannada used in the opening: "Enlaa" (so, what, teasing), "biddu eddu" (fell and got up), "darshana aaytaa?" (did you get darshan?), "kano" (to a male friend), "maga" (buddy), "Hogo!" (get lost), "cooling glass" (the Bangalore English for sunglasses), "Line hinde nillu" (stay behind the line), "Hindhe hogo" (get back), "Saaku" (enough), "Ayyo, biddbitta paapa" (oh, he fell, poor thing), "hodedaadtidaare" (they're fighting). No internet-era slang ("scene", "bro", "literally").
- The film unit's Tamil: "Reel kaali aayiduchu" (the reel's finished), "Naalaikki thaan varum" (it'll only come tomorrow).
- The game shows dialogue as it is said (romanised) with an English line under it. This avoids needing Tamil and Kannada fonts on every phone and lets non-speakers follow. **Native-speaker review of every line is still needed.**
