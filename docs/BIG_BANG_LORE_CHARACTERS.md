# BIG BANG LORE: Raja and Lal

**Name change:** the second protagonist, first written as "Ramanna (Rama)", is now **Lal**. The old name appears nowhere in the active game.

**Language rule:** everyone in the game speaks Kannada (2007 Bengaluru, colloquial) except the film crew, who use Tamil or English as a real unit would.

**Status: a proposal for the author's approval.** Everything here is invented for the game **[F]**. Nothing beyond the opening sequence is built. The plot is deliberately left open, as the brief asks: this document gives each man a life of his own and suggests where friction could come from. It does not decide the story.

The opening shows only behaviour: Raja shouting "Thalaiva!" in the crush, Lal getting up and dusting himself off, a smile, a stare, a punch, two half-answers. Nothing below is said on screen yet.

## Raja

**Age 24.** From **Srirampuram**, the old working-class neighbourhood between Malleshwaram and Majestic. A Kannadiga Bangalore boy: he speaks street Kannada, quick and loud, all "maga", "kano" and "guru", with a few English words when he wants to sound bigger. He calls Rajinikanth "Thalaivar" as every fan does.

**Look (reference: Shankar Nag):** lean, medium height, a long oval face with high cheekbones, a strong straight nose and intense deep-set eyes under heavy brows. Dense black hair, swept to the side, full over the ears. A thick moustache. A khaki-beige half-sleeve shirt open at the collar, tucked into dark trousers, and a black strap watch.

## Lal

**Age 27.** He is from **Mathikere**, the old village-turned-locality right beside MSR Nagar. His family is Kannadiga and has been there since before the college. He speaks slow, deliberate Kannada: few words, no slang he doesn't need, and he lets a sentence hang.

**Look (reference: Ambareesh):** big and broad, taller than Raja, a round full face with a wide jaw and full cheeks, a broad nose, a big crown of wavy hair, and a thick moustache. A dark full-sleeve sweatshirt over jeans, and a gold watch.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Family** | His father, Lingappa, a quiet man who drove a lorry for twenty years, has a bad back and is at home now. His mother, Gowramma, runs the house and the family's two rented-out rooms. His elder sister is married in Tumkur. **Lal is the earning son.** |
| **Money** | Steady but stretched. He supports his parents, pays his sister's in-laws' "occasional" demands, and has a small savings book at the cooperative bank that he guards. |
| **Education** | B.Com by correspondence, finished late, while working. He is quietly proud of the certificate and never mentions it. |
| **Work** | He runs the **canteen counter inside the MSRIT campus** on contract, a sub-lease from the main canteen contractor. He knows the students, the staff, the watchmen, the professors' tea orders. He is *inside* the campus, which is why the police take him further in, not out. |
| **Daily routine** | Opens at 6:30. The rush at 10:30 and 1:00. The accounts at night by tube-light. Sunday is the only free day: an oil bath, then a film. |
| **Social circle** | His canteen helper and friend (the "assistant/friend" of the opening; unnamed for now). The Mathikere fan club, which he quietly funds more than he admits. The campus watchmen. Students who owe him tea money. |
| **Ambition** | A proper hotel of his own on New BEL Road, with his father's name on the board. Respect: to be the man people come to when something has to be settled. |
| **Insecurities** | That he is a shopkeeper in a place full of future engineers. That the students' politeness is pity. That he has never done anything *big*. |
| **Ego** | Quiet, deep and slow to show. He never shouts first. When he does move, he will not stop. He keeps score. |
| **Sense of justice** | Procedural: things done properly, debts paid, order kept, your word kept. He believes loyalty is *carried*: you protect your people quietly and do not perform it. |
| **Rajinikanth** | Thalaivar is discipline and dignity. The man who stayed simple. Lal watches the films for the style and the restraint, and he disapproves of fans who make a circus. (He is still a die-hard. He just thinks he does it *right*.) |
| **Strengths** | Patience, reliability, nerve, a sense of fairness, people trust him with money. |
| **Weaknesses** | Unforgiving, rigid, secretly vain, and contemptuous of anyone he thinks is "all noise". |
| **Contradictions** | He preaches restraint and holds grudges for years. He is proud of being humble. He is a peace-keeper who enjoys being feared. |
| **Bengaluru** | He has watched his village become a city: fields turned into hostels, the ORR cutting through. He belongs to a place that is disappearing under the new one. |
| **Gait / body** | Broad and heavier, with a deliberate stride, chest out and arms quiet. He smiles before he speaks. |

## Why they could hate each other (options for the author to choose from, or replace)

These are **not decided** and are not in the game. Each gives both men a legitimate side.

1. **The banner and the theatre.** Their two fan clubs (Srirampuram and Mathikere) are both promised the same theatre frontage for *Sivaji*'s first-day banner and cut-out. Raja's club paid first; Lal's club has the theatre manager's word. Each is right by his own idea of loyalty.
2. **The sister.** Raja's sister Kavya studies at MSRIT. Something happened at Lal's canteen counter: a debt, an accusation, a protective act that Raja misread. Lal thinks he did the decent thing. Raja thinks Lal humiliated his family.
3. **The cable line.** Raja's operator's cable runs over Mathikere roofs, Lal's area. Lal had it cut (he says it was illegal and dangerous; a boy was hurt). It cost Raja his job for a month.
4. **An old fight.** They have met before, at a first-day show years ago, and only one of them remembers why it started.

## The relationship: principles, not plot

- They are never "friends" in the easy sense. They can stand next to each other and still argue.
- Each will do something for the other that he will never explain and the other will never thank him for.
- Their speech stays sparse: behaviour, silence and the cost of a choice carry the weight.
- The audience should be able to take either man's side in any given scene.
- *Sivaji*'s release on 15 June 2007 is where their two loyalties, to Thalaivar, to their people and to each other, have to be tested in public.
